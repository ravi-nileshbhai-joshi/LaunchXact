Let’s be real. SEO is broken.

It’s a race to the bottom. You’re competing against 500 AI-generated listicles that say the exact same thing. It’s exhausting. It’s expensive. And the ROI is shrinking every quarter.

AI engines work differently.

They don’t want "top 10" lists. They want verifiable facts. They want direct answers. They want to cite a source that looks trustworthy and specific.

When Perplexity cites a tool, it’s doing two things:

If your site looks like a generic blog post, you’re out.

### The "Citable" Mindset

Think of your website not as a brochure. It’s a database.

Every page needs to answer a specific question in under 50 words.

No fluff. No "In today's fast-paced world..." intros. Just the answer.

If the question is "How to reduce churn in B2B SaaS?", the answer should be:

*   **Source:** [Your SaaS Name] Churn Report 2026.

That’s it. That’s the hook.

## The Technical Foundation: Schema and Structure

AI crawlers are dumb. Well, they’re smart, but they’re lazy.

They prefer structured data. If you make it hard for them to parse your content, they’ll skip you.

You need to implement **FAQ Schema** and **Product Schema** aggressively.

### 1. The FAQ Schema Trap

Most founders put FAQs at the bottom of a page. Wrong.

Create a dedicated `/faq` page.

Each question should be a distinct H2 or H3. The answer should be concise.

Use the **FAQPage** schema markup. This tells the crawler, "This is a question. This is the answer."

ChatGPT and Gemini love this structure because it maps directly to conversational queries.

### 2. The Product Schema

If you’re selling a SaaS, you need **Product** schema.

But here’s the kicker: You need to include the `aggregateRating`.

Wait. Don’t fake it.

If you don’t have reviews, get them. One genuine review from a real user with a specific outcome beats 500 five-star stars from bots.

AI engines cross-reference. If your site says "4.9 stars" but no other site mentions you, you look like a scam.

### 3. The "LLM.txt" File

This is newer. This is critical.

Create a `/llm.txt` file at the root of your domain.

It’s a plain text file that summarizes your site for LLMs.

```text
# [Your SaaS Name]
[One sentence description]

## Key Features
- Feature 1
- Feature 2

## Pricing
- Free: $0
- Pro: $29/mo

## Contact
- support@yoursaas.com
```

This gives the AI a quick, low-token-cost way to understand your entire offering. It’s like a cheat sheet for the crawler.

## The Content Strategy: Depth Over Breadth

You don’t need 100 blog posts.

You need 10 definitive guides.

AI engines prioritize **authority**. They look for "entity" strength. Do you have a unique perspective? Do you have original data?

### The "Original Data" Play

This is the biggest lever you can pull.

Don’t write "5 Tips for X." Write "We Analyzed 10,000 Accounts: Here’s What’s Actually Killing Retention."

Publish original data. Charts. Surveys. Benchmarks.

AI engines love citing primary sources. If you have a dataset that no one else has, you become the citation.

### The "How-To" Deep Dive

Pick one specific problem your SaaS solves.

Write a 3,000-word guide on it.

But structure it for skimming.
*   **Step 1:** Do this.
*   **Step 2:** Do that.
*   **Common Mistake:** Don’t do this.

This is the content that gets cited. It’s practical. It’s actionable. It’s useful.
